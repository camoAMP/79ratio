# IT Is Slowing the Business Down

## Entry

### Headline
When IT slows the business down, something fundamental is misaligned.

### Sub-headline
Not because people aren't working hard—but because systems weren't designed for how you actually operate.

---

## What This Looks Like

When technology becomes a constraint instead of an enabler, friction appears everywhere:

- Simple requests take longer than they should to fulfill.
- Teams build workarounds because the official path feels slower.
- Decisions require too many approval loops and stakeholder conversations.
- Changes feel risky because dependencies aren't clear.
- IT stays busy but organizational momentum doesn't increase.

---

## The Real Cause

### Headline
IT slows businesses when systems evolve without intention.

As organizations grow, technology expands reactively—tools layered under pressure, integrations patched to "make it work," processes adjusted to fit constraints. Over time, the environment optimizes for preventing problems rather than enabling possibilities. The result: friction that slows everything down.

---

## Why This Matters

- Opportunities take longer to act on.
- Teams hesitate to improve processes because change feels risky.
- Leadership avoids technology decisions that require IT involvement.
- Innovation becomes exhausting instead of exciting.
- Technology becomes a constraint on strategy rather than a lever for it.

---

## The 79 Ratio Perspective

Technology should accelerate your business, not constrain it. When systems are intentionally designed, IT becomes an operational asset—enabling teams to move faster, make clearer decisions, and act with confidence. Reactive design creates friction that compounds over time.

---

## What Changes

When technology is designed for business enablement:

- Decisions happen faster because information is clear and accessible.
- Workarounds disappear because the official path actually works.
- Low-risk improvements happen because systems connect predictably.
- Leadership gains flexibility to pursue opportunities without technology delays.

---

## Should You Act?

Consider this relevant if:

- Requests feel slow to complete.
- Your teams rely on workarounds and manual processes.
- IT effort isn't creating visible organizational momentum.
- Growth creates friction instead of opportunity.
